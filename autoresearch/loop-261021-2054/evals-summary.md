# Development-loop review

Eight bounded Autoresearch trials used the same 150 paired seeds (300 full matches), seed 20261202, Hardest vs Harder with Medium partners. These outcomes selected the candidate and are not held-out evidence of strength.

The development baseline scored 50.3333%; the retained version scored 52.6667%, a 2.3333 percentage-point increase. The frozen previous Hardest scored 50.6667% on the same training deals. Three trials were retained and five discarded. All correctness guards passed; the final primary project passed 189 tests and its production build.

The partner-policy tactical forecast contributed +1.3333 percentage points to the fixed development metric. Planning one trick earlier contributed another +1.0 point, with higher computation per sampled deal. Exact pruning and settled-score shortcuts preserved every one of the incumbent's 300 match outcomes and decision counts, while concurrent mean decision time fell from 185.3ms to 164.6ms (11.2%). The timing comparison is diagnostic, not a controlled latency benchmark.

Dealer-call inference tied the metric and added complexity; posterior resample-move refinement, removing play-choice evidence, a pure tactical forecast, and modeling simple play by opponents underperformed the incumbent on the development set. They were discarded. Choosing among noisy development measurements can overfit, so a frozen, fresh-seed benchmark is required.

Held-out validation was fixed before observing its results: seed 20261221, 500 paired seeds per setup, 6,000 current-ladder matches plus 2,000 frozen-old-Hardest control matches against unchanged Harder. The two complete builds share the engine and policies; only the candidate-only tactical helper differs after an independently checked implementation speedup. Both exact builds are archived. Development samples are not pooled with validation. All difficulties retain their previous sample budgets; only Hardest's strategy changes. Globally perfect hidden-information play is not claimed.
